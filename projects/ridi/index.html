Identical audits, different AI decisions

Same reported audit. Different selected identities. Different downstream behavior.

RIDI studies allocation identity at the score-to-action boundary: when scores become a finite queue, shortlist or context window, which entities actually receive the scarce slots — and is that membership identified by the audit we report?

The scientific object

Allocation identity is not another performance metric.

Performance, group fairness, calibration and robustness remain necessary. Allocation identity asks a different question: whether the identities receiving finite action are specified, stable and justified under the comparison of interest.

Measure

Who changed?

Compare equal-capacity selected sets directly rather than infer membership stability from rank correlation or aggregate counts.

Attribute

What changed them?

Use matched controls, representation interventions, natural updates and preregistered tests to delimit mechanisms.

Control

How much change is necessary?

Certify zero-turnover cases from score margins and bound avoidable turnover under an explicit utility-regret budget.

Decisive preregistered test

RAG: exact retrieval audit, non-equivalent behavior.

Frozen design800 queries across Natural Questions, HotpotQA, FEVER and SciFact. Every positive-qrel passage stayed at the same rank; only metric-zero identities changed.
Audit equalityPrecision@k, recall@k, nDCG@k, MRR@k, MAP@k and the complete relevance-grade-by-position vector were identical by construction; 24,500 real-data checks had zero failures.
Primary resultEqual-dataset-weight macro canonical-output change: 32.87%. Benchmark-defined correctness flips: 17.27% (95% stratified-bootstrap interval 14.60–20.03%).
Mechanism controlsOrder-only permutation: 4.8% macro correctness flips. Identity-dose ladder: 6.97% → 11.07% → 17.27% as realized RIDI increased 0.446 → 0.655 → 0.953.
Concrete caseSciFact claim 275 changes SUPPORTS → REFUTES when nine metric-zero passage identities are replaced, while the same-query order-only control remains SUPPORTS.
Cross-system programme

Structural result, operational tests, explicit boundaries.

COMPASConstructive audit-equivalent cohorts show that progressively refined group-count audits can strongly constrain membership without necessarily identifying it.
EPSSA production v2→v3 update replaced 565 of the top 1,000 remediation priorities versus 0 and 7 in adjacent same-version controls; delayed external value was cutoff-dependent.
CMS HVBPAnnual Total Performance Score updates provide a second independently governed production scoring system for transport.
Registered failuresRxNorm and Open Targets results are retained to delimit prevalence, effect size and external value. The theorem is general; consequential turnover is not claimed to be universal.
