Allocation-identity research programme

Identical audits can yield different AI decisions

Same declared audit. Different finite identities. Sometimes a different downstream decision.

RIDI studies allocation identity at the score-to-action boundary: when scores become a finite queue, shortlist or context window, which entities actually receive the scarce slots — and is that membership identified by the audit we report?

The scientific object

Allocation identity is not another performance metric.

Performance, group fairness, calibration and robustness remain necessary. Allocation identity asks a different question: whether the identities receiving finite action are specified by the evaluation and how much membership changes between compared states.

Measure

Who changed?

Compare equal-capacity selected sets directly instead of inferring membership stability from global rank correlation or aggregate counts.

Certify

Can zero turnover be guaranteed?

Use the sufficient score-margin certificate when stored paired scores make top-k stability provable.

Control

How much change is necessary?

Use the exact identity–utility frontier to minimize turnover under an explicit updated-score utility-regret budget.

Decisive preregistered test

RAG: exact retrieval audit, non-equivalent behavior.

Frozen design800 queries across Natural Questions, HotpotQA, FEVER and SciFact. Every positive-qrel passage stayed at the same rank; only metric-zero identities changed.
Audit equalityPrecision@k, recall@k, nDCG@k, MRR@k, MAP@k and the complete relevance-grade-by-position vector were identical by construction; 24,500 real-data and 72,000 synthetic checks had zero mismatches.
Primary resultEqual-dataset-weight macro canonical-output divergence: 32.87%. Benchmark-defined correctness divergence: 17.27% (95% stratified-bootstrap interval 14.60–20.03%). Most outcomes remained stable.
Mechanism controlsOrder-only permutation: 4.80% macro correctness divergence. Identity-dose ladder: 6.97% → 11.07% → 17.27% as realized RIDI increased 0.446 → 0.655 → 0.953. No clean low-dose causal decomposition is claimed.
Concrete caseSciFact claim 275 changes SUPPORTS → REFUTES when nine metric-zero passage identities are replaced, while the same-query order-only control remains SUPPORTS.

Reproducibility status

What has been rerun, and what has not.

The prospective RAG protocol, panels, model revisions, prompts, interventions and analysis plan were frozen publicly before registered generation. The sealed EPSS workflow has two independent external reproductions. A targeted blind external regeneration of SciFact 275 on a distinct GPU/software stack reproduced the substantive SUPPORTS versus REFUTES identity-substitution pattern, with a transparent strict-parser boundary on the regenerated reference prefix.
