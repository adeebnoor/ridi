Selection identity and the identity–utility frontier

RIDI studies what changes when a ranking system is updated: which identities enter or leave the finite action set, and how much of that turnover was actually required by the updated scoring objective.

Research question

Aggregate evaluation can summarize quality without uniquely identifying selected membership. The preregistered RAG experiment tests this under exact equality of the complete assessed relevance-grade sequence and five standard retrieval measures. The constructive contribution then moves beyond diagnosis: the identity–utility frontier computes the exact minimum replacement count compatible with a declared rank-utility budget.

Evidence

RAG: 17.27% benchmark-defined correctness changes across 800 preregistered queries under exact retrieval-audit equality. Changes run in both directions. No primary query had uniquely identified passage membership.
EPSS: a production v2→v3 update changed 565 of the top 1,000 priorities. At a 0.01% rank-utility budget, the frontier avoided 14.34% of those replacements while retaining all 12 later KEV hits recovered by the updated list.
Cross-domain frontier: at the same 0.1% budget, mean avoidable turnover was 78.8% in the GraphSAGE application and 28.7% in 20 Newsgroups retrieval.

Boundaries

RIDI does not equate turnover with harm, unfairness or error. The EPSS outcomes are sparse and retrospective. Exploratory drug-interaction analyses are not clinical validation. A post hoc parser sensitivity is reported separately from the registered RAG endpoint, and no longer-generation sensitivity has yet been completed. No formal CODECHECK certificate has been issued.
