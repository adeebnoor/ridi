Identical audits can yield different AI decisions

A technically correct audit can still leave the finite allocation on which an AI system acts unidentified.

The manuscript tests allocation identity as a distinct object of evaluation at the score-to-action boundary. Its decisive prospective experiment asks whether two RAG contexts can have exactly the same declared retrieval audit yet differ in downstream behavior because different passage identities occupy the finite context.

The identity–utility frontier bounds avoidable turnover under an explicit utility budget.

The exact frontier computes the minimum membership change compatible with a declared updated-score utility-regret budget. It uses stored scores and requires no retraining.
