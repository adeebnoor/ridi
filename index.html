The missing estimand

Audit the score-to-action boundary.

RIDI treats allocation identity as a directly observable evaluation axis for systems that convert scores into finite actions. It complements performance, fairness, calibration, and robustness rather than replacing them.

Performance
How well does the system predict or rank?
Group fairness
How are outcomes distributed across groups?
Calibration / robustness
Are scores reliable or stable under specified conditions?
Allocation identity
Who receives finite action — and who changed?

Research agenda

Use RIDI to try to break the idea.

The most useful next studies are not repetitions of the existing examples. They test where allocation identity matters, where it collapses to existing quantities, and when it predicts consequences that aggregate metrics miss.

New domains

Clinical review queues, vulnerability remediation, fraud investigation, hiring, inspections, document review, grants, and any finite-attention pipeline.

Prospective tests

Lock a capacity and selection rule before a model or data update; measure identity turnover and connect it to a temporally later outcome.

Boundary cases

Find systems where aggregate evaluation already identifies membership, or where turnover is scientifically irrelevant. Negative results are informative.

Methods

Develop alternative estimators, uncertainty intervals, certificates, controls, and allocation-aware governance or reporting standards.

Work with the project

Bring a dataset, a counterexample, or a better estimator.

A strong contribution can confirm the phenomenon, delimit it, falsify a proposed mechanism, or show that another allocation-aware quantity is better. The project is designed for independent use and critique.

Scientific status

Open by design. Claims bounded.

RIDI is research software accompanying a manuscript in preparation/submission workflow. The repository exposes code, controls, negative results, and reproducibility material so researchers can inspect the claim rather than rely on a landing page.

Open sourcePublic codeInteractive experimentNegative results retainedIndependent checking invited

No claim of journal acceptance, external certification, or universal empirical prevalence is made here. Allocation identity is proposed as a distinct estimand specifically at finite-action boundaries.
