RIDI studies a missing question at the score-to-action boundary: who actually receives finite action — and who changed?

Many AI systems are evaluated with performance, calibration, ranking, and group-level summaries, yet deployment often ends in a finite shortlist, queue, review set, or top-k action. Allocation identity is the membership of that finite action set. RIDI provides an open toolkit to measure, stress-test, reproduce, and control that quantity.

The idea

Performance asks how well a system predicts. Fairness asks how outcomes are distributed across groups. Allocation identity asks which entities receive the scarce action. These are related but not interchangeable scientific objects.

Aggregate audits can identify cells without identifying their members.

RIDI — the Reproducibility of Identity Decisions Index — is one operational measure of allocation difference. It is not a replacement for AUROC, precision, recall, calibration, fairness, robustness, or causal analysis.

Evidence programme

The empirical programme asks where allocation identity is large, small, useful, redundant, or consequential. Negative results remain part of the record.

COMPASConstructive audit-equivalent allocations show that matched aggregate performance and group summaries can still leave many individual identities unresolved.
EPSSProduction-version changes provide a cybersecurity test of finite top-k identity turnover, with matched controls and a prospective follow-up protocol.
CMS HVBPA second independently governed production scoring system tests whether the allocation question transports beyond cybersecurity.
Controlled testsRepresentation and model interventions probe mechanisms that can move finite allocations even when conventional summaries move little.
Registered failuresRxNorm and Open Targets analyses are retained to delimit empirical prevalence and prevent the theorem from being confused with a universal turnover claim.
