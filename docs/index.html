Allocation identity,
from question to test.

This guide is for researchers who want to audit a finite-action AI pipeline, reproduce RIDI analyses, design a prospective test, or challenge the underlying claim.

The shortest versionIf a model ultimately selects only k people, cases, alerts, vulnerabilities, documents, or institutions, report not only how well it scores—but whether the identities receiving those k actions are stable.

1. Research question

Most evaluation pipelines observe summaries of outcomes, groups, scores, or ranks. RIDI asks a different question at the score-to-action boundary:

Who receives the finite action — and who changed?

The identification claim is deliberately narrower than “all AI audits are insufficient.” If an audit factors through finite outcome/group/rank-position cells, fixing the audited cell counts need not identify which members occupy those cells. Allocation-aware, individual, or causal audits can retain information that aggregate summaries discard.

2. Quickstart

Define the action capacity.

Choose a real operational capacity k when one exists. If k is analytical rather than operational, label it explicitly.

Freeze the selection rule.

Specify score direction, tie handling, exclusions, threshold/top-k logic, and version timestamps before comparing allocations.

Extract identities.

For two versions, obtain the selected identity sets A and B at equal capacity.

Measure turnover.

Report overlap, changed slots, RIDI, and the conventional performance/ranking metrics relevant to your field.

Add controls and an outcome gate.

Use relabel/no-change or within-version controls where appropriate. For prospective work, lock the external outcome and temporal direction in advance.

# minimal set-level calculation A = set(selected_ids_version_0) B = set(selected_ids_version_1) ridi = 1 - len(A & B) / len(A | B) changed_slots = len(A - B) # equal-capacity top-k print({"RIDI": ridi, "changed_slots": changed_slots})

High-value applications

Look for pipelines where attention is genuinely scarce: clinical alert review, cybersecurity remediation, fraud or compliance investigation, inspection, hiring, grant review, document triage, or any budget-constrained ranking. A domain is scientifically useful when the identities matter operationally—not merely because a top-k can be computed.

5. Interpret without overclaiming

High rank correlation does not imply identity stability. It summarizes rank association, not finite-set membership. Conversely, non-zero RIDI does not by itself establish harm, unfairness, or poor model quality. It establishes that the finite allocation changed. The scientific question is whether that change is expected, consequential, controllable, or informative in the domain.

RIDI is one instrument for allocation identity. If another measure better matches your decision geometry, use it—and report why. The project’s stronger claim concerns observability of allocation membership, not ownership of a particular metric.

7. Contribute like a researcher

The project welcomes four kinds of contribution: independent replication, a new-domain application, a boundary/counterexample, or a methodological extension. A useful issue should state the decision object, capacity, comparator, what conventional audit remains stable or changes, and what allocation-aware result you observe.

8. Scientific boundaries

Allocation identity is proposed for capacity-limited score-to-action systems. It is not a substitute for performance, group fairness, causal analysis, calibration, robustness, or domain-specific safety evaluation. Constructive audit-equivalent allocations establish non-identification; they are not predictions that a deployed model would choose every compatible allocation. Empirical prevalence and downstream consequence must be tested separately.

A good falsification is a contribution.If your domain shows that allocation identity is redundant, immaterial, or already identified by its standard audit, document it. The goal is to establish the boundary of the estimand, not to manufacture positive turnover.
